Neuroscience studies the structure and function of the nervous system, from molecular pathways to complex cognitive behaviors. Neurons communicate via electrochemical signals across synapses, using neurotransmitters such as glutamate, GABA, and dopamine. Advanced imaging techniques, including fMRI and two-photon microscopy, allow researchers to observe brain activity in real time with high spatial resolution. Neural plasticity — the brain's ability to reorganize synaptic connections — underlies learning, memory formation, and recovery from injury. Neurodegenerative disorders like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease involve progressive loss of neuronal function and are associated with protein misfolding and inflammatory responses. Brain-computer interfaces now enable direct communication between neural tissue and external devices, offering promising avenues for restoring motor function in paralyzed patients. Computational neuroscience integrates experimental data with mathematical models to simulate neural networks and decipher the neural code underlying perception, decision-making, and consciousness.
