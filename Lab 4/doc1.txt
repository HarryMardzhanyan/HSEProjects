Astrophysics investigates the physical processes governing the universe. It focuses on stars, galaxies, and the interstellar medium. Through spectral analysis, astrophysicists determine the chemical composition, temperature, and radial velocity of celestial objects. A key observable phenomenon is redshift, which helps estimate distances to remote galaxies and confirms the expansion of space. Modern observatories detect the cosmic microwave background radiation — the residual electromagnetic field from the epoch of recombination. Dark matter and dark energy remain fundamental puzzles, as they do not interact with light yet influence the gravitational dynamics of galaxy clusters. Modeling thermonuclear nucleosynthesis in stellar cores reveals the origin of elements heavier than helium. Next-generation space telescopes extend the observable horizon, discovering exoplanets within habitable zones and analyzing their atmospheric compositions. Gravitational wave detectors now provide a new window into cataclysmic events like black hole mergers, complementing traditional electromagnetic observations.
