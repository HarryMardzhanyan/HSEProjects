Climate science examines long-term patterns and changes in Earth's atmospheric, oceanic, and terrestrial systems. Researchers use climate models to simulate interactions between greenhouse gases, aerosols, cloud cover, and surface albedo. Paleoclimate data from ice cores, tree rings, and sediment layers provide context for current warming trends. The enhanced greenhouse effect, driven primarily by anthropogenic CO2 emissions, contributes to rising global temperatures, sea-level rise, and extreme weather frequency. Ocean acidification, resulting from absorbed carbon dioxide, threatens marine ecosystems and coral reefs. Feedback loops, such as permafrost thawing and reduced Arctic ice reflectivity, may accelerate climate change beyond linear projections. International assessments, like those from the IPCC, synthesize peer-reviewed research to inform policy decisions. Mitigation strategies include renewable energy adoption, carbon capture technologies, and sustainable land-use practices, while adaptation measures focus on infrastructure resilience and ecosystem conservation.
