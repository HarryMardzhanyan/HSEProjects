Molecular biology explores the mechanisms of genetic information storage and expression at the macromolecular level. The central dogma describes the flow of genetic data from DNA to messenger RNA and then to polypeptide chains. Transcription and translation processes are coordinated by enzymatic complexes, including RNA polymerases, spliceosomes, and ribosomes. Next-generation sequencing enables high-throughput reading of nucleotide sequences with minimal error rates. The CRISPR-Cas9 system allows precise genome editing, revolutionizing functional genetics and therapeutic development. Epigenetic modifications, such as DNA methylation and histone acetylation, regulate gene activity without altering the underlying sequence. Protein folding, governed by chaperone molecules, determines biological function, while misfolding can lead to neurodegenerative diseases. Single-cell RNA sequencing now reveals cellular heterogeneity within tissues, advancing our understanding of development, immunity, and cancer progression.
